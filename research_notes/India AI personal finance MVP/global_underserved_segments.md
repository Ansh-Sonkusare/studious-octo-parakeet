# Underserved Segments and Cross-Border Gaps in Global Personal Finance Where an AI "End-Result" Product Could Win (as of 7 Oct 2026)

Method note: evidence was gathered through web searches; several figures come from secondary coverage (news, vendor press releases) rather than the primary report, and this is flagged per item. Where I found no source, the item is in Gaps. "Inferences" are my own reasoning, not sourced facts, and regulatory comments there come from general knowledge, not from fetched sources. ## 1. Diaspora and cross-border: who is hurting, how big is it, who competes, what is missing?

### Takeaway
Remittance rails are huge (about $729B to LMICs in 2025) and well served by Wise and Remitly. The newly funded layer is "beyond the transfer": Aspora, Félix and Abound are moving into investing, savings, credit and AI assistants. The least-served, highest-friction piece appears to be cross-border tax and compliance for NRIs (PFIC/FBAR/TDS refunds/DTAA), plus cross-border estate and inheritance, where I found no dedicated product evidence.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and flows**
- Remittance inflows to low- and middle-income countries reached $728.6B in 2025 (IFAD figure, up 94% from 2016 to 2025). I did not find the World Bank's own 2025 country-level data. — [UN/IFAD coverage via APP](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=1277230); search summary of 2024 country data also at [Wikipedia: remittances received](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_remittances_received)
- The UN's November 2025 briefing says recorded remittances to LMICs reached nearly $700B in 2024, up 10.0% after 1.2% growth in 2023. — [UN DESA WESP Nov 2025](https://policy.desa.un.org/publications/world-economic-situation-and-prospects-november-2025-briefing-no-196)
- 2024 inflows by country (World Bank series): India about $137.7B, Mexico about $67.6B, Philippines about $40.3B (8.7% of GDP), Nigeria about $21.3B (about 11.3% of GDP, ranked 10th). — [Wikipedia: remittances received](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_remittances_received), [Nairametrics](https://nairametrics.com/?p=82442)
- RBI recorded a record $135.46B of remittances for FY2024-25, a 14% rise. This series uses Indian fiscal years, so it differs from the World Bank's calendar-year figure. — [AIR/NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/remittances-by-indians-working-abroad-scale-record-high-of-135-billion-in-fy25/)
- The IOM World Migration Report 2026 (as relayed by a secondary source) puts the US as the top source country, sending $103.2B out. — [American Bazaar](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/05/27/us-leads-global-remittance-outflows-india-tops-recipient-with-137b-481665/)
- Overseas Indians (NRIs plus PIOs) number about 3.43 crore (about 34.3 million) as of January 2025, per MEA data as summarised by a secondary source. — [Indian News Link NZ](https://indiannewslink.co.nz/malayalis-lead-indias-nri-wealth-inflows); MEA source file: [MEA LS196_A](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/CPV/LS196_A.pdf). Sources disagree; no reliable NRI-only count was found.
- One article citing RBI/MEA data puts NRI deposits in Indian banks at $263.7B (Oct 2024, up 13%) and FCNR deposits at $52.5B, but the same article uses the figure inconsistently. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/look-nris-investing-india-18833.html/) (treat with caution)
- Global average cost of sending $200 was 6.49% in Q1 2025 (up from 6.26% in Q4 2024); the "SmaRT" cheapest-service average was 3.29%; the SDG target is below 3% by 2030. — [World Bank Remittance Prices Worldwide Q1 2025](https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/sites/default/files/rpw_main_report_and_annex_q125_1_0.pdf)

**Incumbents and entrants (company-reported scale)**
- Wise FY2026 (year to March 2026): 18.9M active customers (+21%), cross-border volume $243.5B (+31%), net revenue about $2.5B, take rate fell to 0.52%. — [Wise FY26 results via Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/wise-class-a--wise/wise-fy26-results/9637901)
- Remitly FY2025: send volume $74.9B (+37%), revenue $1.6B (+29%), 9.3M active customers, first full year of GAAP profit ($67.9M). — [Remitly Q4/FY2025 release](https://remitly.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/remitly-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-above)
- Aspora (formerly Vance): $53M Series B (June 2025) co-led by Sequoia and Greylock, about $93M raised in total; says over 250,000 NRIs and over $2B volume (another outlet says 400,000+ users, so user counts conflict); operates in the UK, EU, UAE; planned US, Australia, Canada, Singapore; adding mutual funds and a "full-stack neobank for immigrants". — [FintechFutures](https://www.fintechfutures.com/venture-capital-funding/remittance-platform-aspora-raises-53m-series-b), [Aspora announcement](https://get.aspora.com/news/series-b), [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/sequoia-and-greylock-lead-50-mn-round-in-aspora-9367516)
- Félix Pago (WhatsApp-native remittances for Latino immigrants): reported $200M Series B on 1 Sept 2026 ($87M equity led by a16z plus a $113M credit facility from General Catalyst); more than $8B transferred, 6M+ people, 11 countries; settles via USDC on Stellar; funds aimed at lending, savings and an AI financial assistant. Figures come from secondary reporting of a Bloomberg story and are company-stated. An earlier $75M Series B (QED) was reported in April 2025. — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/felix-pagos-200m-series-b-a16z-validates-stablecoin-rails-for-latam-immigrants/), [Cryptorank](https://cryptorank.io/insights/deals/felix-payments-extended-series-b-2026-09-01), [LatamList](https://latamlist.com/felix-pago-raises-75m-series-b-to-expand-cross-border-payments-in-latam/)
- Belong (NRI app): $5M seed led by Elevation; USD fixed deposits via GIFT City partner banks, IFSCA PSP and broker-dealer licences; roadmap of mutual funds, equities and insurance. — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/belong-launches-nri-app-raises-5m), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/corporate-news/uae-nris-get-digital-access-to-indian-investments-without-visiting-a-bank-1.500284317)
- Abound (US NRI app): $14M seed (May 2025, led by NEAR Foundation), over $150M remitted; announced in April 2026 a planned AI "financial autopilot" with NEAR AI for FX, NRE/NRO balances and recurring payments. This is a plan, not a launched feature. — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/abound-raises-14m-for-nri-fintech), [IBS Intelligence](https://ibsintelligence.com/ibsi-news/abound-near-ai-build-ai-financial-autopilot-for-nris/)

**NRI tax and compliance pain (blogs and fund-house content, not authoritative)**
- Some Indian AMCs restrict or complicate NRI investment from the US and Canada because of FBAR/PFIC burdens. — [Equities India glossary](https://equitiesindia.com/glossary/mutual-fund-taxation-nri), [SBNRI](https://sbnri.com/blog/education/nri-challenges-direct-mutual-funds-india)
- Indian mutual funds are PFICs for US taxpayers (Form 8621); one guide says CPA fees of $100 to $300 per form add up and that switches or transfers can be treated as "constructive sales". This is a blog, so verify with a US tax professional. — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/pfic-rules-for-indian-nris-in-usa-tax-impact-solutions/)
- On the India side, TDS is deducted on the full gain before exemptions, refunds only come after an ITR-2 filing, small TDS credits scatter across AMCs, and common errors include wrong residential status and omitted NRE/NRO interest. — [AskFuzz](https://askfuzz.ai/discover/news/personal-finance/nri-mf-tax-tds-gap-creates-additional-burden), [HDFC MF guide](https://www.hdfcfund.com/learn/blog/how-file-income-tax-nris-comprehensive-guide), [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/a-tax-planning-mistake-many-middle-east-nris-make-with-indian-mutual-funds/)

### Inferences
- The remittance transfer itself is a commoditised, rate-compressing business (Wise take rate 0.52%, Remitly scale). Value is shifting to attached products (investing, credit, savings), which well-funded players (Aspora, Félix, Abound) are now building. A small team should not compete on the transfer or on neobank licensing.
- The un-owned niche is "compliance and consolidation as an outcome": a service that ingests NRE/NRO statements, CAMS/KFintech CAS statements, US brokerage 1099s and Form 16A, then outputs a filed or CPA-ready India ITR plus US 8621/FBAR/FATCA packet, a DTAA credit calculation and a TDS refund claim. Nothing I found does this end to end. Tax-prep content marketing (SBNRI-type) appears to be the current alternative, but I did not verify SBNRI's product scope.
- Each segment (US-India, UK-India, Gulf-India) has different rules, so the wedge is probably the US-resident NRI (highest PFIC pain, highest income). India-based founders hold a structural advantage on the India-side filing, data access (CAS, AIS/26AS) and CA/CPA labour costs.
- Regulatory friction is high where the product touches money movement or advice (RBI/FEMA, SEBI RIA rules, IRS Circular 230 for practitioners). Document-ingestion plus a CA/CPA-reviewed filing keeps it closer to a tax-preparation service than a regulated financial intermediary.
- Cross-border inheritance (NRI heirs of Indian property or deposits; Filipino/Mexican/Nigerian heirs of foreign accounts) is plausibly very painful but appears under-documented; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- No credible market-size number for NRI investable assets or NRI tax-filing spend; remittance totals are flows, not balances, and should not be used as a proxy for wealth.
- No NRI-only population figure found (sources conflict; 3.43 crore includes PIOs).
- No primary World Bank 2025 country-level remittance data located (Migration and Development Brief December 2025 not retrieved).
- No evidence found on willingness to pay for NRI tax filing (price points of NRI CAs, or what SBNRI-type firms charge).
- No sourced data on cross-border inheritance or estate problems for diasporas.
- No data located on Filipino/Nigerian diaspora investing products (Nigerian and Filipino diaspora competitors not researched).

## 2. Gig, freelancer and creator finance: how large is the problem and what has worked commercially?

### Takeaway
The US independent workforce is about 73M to 75M by MBO's broad definition, but only about 27.6M work full-time at it. The category has a mixed commercial record: Collective reported eight-figure ARR at roughly $299 per month, while Catch shut down in March 2023. Evidence suggests pure "set aside and save" fintechs struggle; tax, bookkeeping and filing services with a concrete deliverable get paid.

### Cited Findings
- MBO Partners 2025 State of Independence (published 9 Sept 2025): 72.9M US independent workers, split 37.4M occasional, 27.6M full-time, 7.9M part-time. — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/number-of-independent-workers-rises-again-mbo-partners), [MBO Partners](https://www.mbopartners.com/state-of-independence)
- A 16th edition is reported (28 Sept 2026) with a record 74.9M independents, but I did not locate the primary MBO release. — [SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/mbo-partners-state-of-independence-2026/)
- Upwork's Future Workforce Index (April 2025): 28% of US knowledge workers (more than 20M) freelance, generating $1.5T in 2024. Its older Freelance Forward study (2023) counted 64M Americans freelancing (38% of the workforce). Definitions differ across Upwork and MBO. — [Barchart/Upwork](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/32003343/upwork-study-finds-1-in-4-u-s-skilled-knowledge-workers-now-work-independently-generating-1-5-trillion-in-earnings), [Stock Titan: Upwork 2023](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/UPWK/upwork-study-finds-64-million-americans-freelanced-in-2023-adding-1-qaay1kokbli3.html)
- Collective raised $50M (July 2023, total $82M); claims "eight-figure" ARR (unaudited); one source lists a $299 monthly membership. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/collective-raises-50m-launch-ai-powered-finance-platform-for-freelancers), [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2023/07/210179-fintech-firm-collective-raises-50m-to-establish-ai-powered-finance-platform-for-freelancers/)
- Catch (tax set-asides, benefits, insurance for freelancers) shut down in March 2023 after raising about $18M, citing market conditions. — [GPA](https://gpa.net/blogs/americas/us-payroll-and-benefits-fintech-catch-calls-it-a-day)
- A 2019 Pew piece reports that about a third of full-time independents say retirement planning is a top challenge (old data). — [Pew](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2019/06/28/what-we-know-and-dont-know-about-independent-workers-and-retirement-savings)
- IRS Direct File was discontinued and is not available in the 2026 filing season; its users doubled from 140,803 returns in 2024 to 296,531 in 2025 but still under 0.5% of returns. Free File remains for AGI of $89,000 or less. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/a-free-tax-filing-option-just-disappeared), [Mondaq](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/unitedstates/tax-authorities/1730108/irs-ends-direct-file-what-us-taxpayers-need-to-know-for-the-2026-filing-season)
- Intuit (fiscal 2025 Q3): TurboTax Live revenue +47%; assisted, AI-augmented tiers drive growth; free-user count expected to fall from 10M to 8M. Newer AI-first entrants include Deduction (US consumers, $2.8M pre-seed, AI with CPA review) and april (embedded tax filing via partners, $30M Series A; date unclear). — [Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/intuit-turbotax-ai-taxpayer-experience-revenue/749052/), [Simplify: Deduction](https://simplify.jobs/c/Deduction), [BuiltInNYC: april](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/april-raises-30m-hiring)

### Inferences
- The workflow people pay for is the end deliverable (filed quarterly estimate, 1099 reconciliation, Schedule C, SEP-IRA/Solo 401(k) setup) rather than budgeting advice. Collective's pricing implies freelancers with meaningful profit will pay hundreds of dollars a month for done-for-you services.
- Catch's failure suggests that carrying insurance or benefits licences and relying on thin interchange or fee revenue is a hard business; an asset-light, software plus human-review model is more suitable for a small team.
- The US is saturated (Intuit, Collective, Keeper, Found, Deduction, april). A differentiated angle for an India-based team could be India's 44ADA presumptive taxation and advance-tax, or global freelancers (India/Philippines/Nigeria-based freelancers paid in USD and needing FIRC/GST/LUT compliance). I found no sourced evidence on the size of those segments, so this is a hypothesis.
- Regulatory friction: moderate. Tax preparation needs PTIN/e-file authority in the US (a licensed partner or CPA reviewer), and India filings generally need CA-supervised or ERI-licensed channels.

### Gaps
- No 2025 numbers on Found, Keeper or Lili (revenue, users); Found and Lili were not found in searches beyond a generic description.
- No retirement-saving rate or underpayment-penalty data for the self-employed (MBO full report gated).
- No UK self-assessment or India 44ADA market size or competitor data gathered.
- No evidence on global freelancers' (non-US) payment/tax compliance pain.

## 3. Retirees, elder finance, caregivers and estate: where is the pain and who pays?

### Takeaway
Elder fraud losses jumped to about $7.7B in 2025 (FBI IC3), and 63M Americans are family caregivers who spend about $7,000+ a year out of pocket. The commercial model that works is B2B/B2B2C (Carefull sells to banks and advisors; Empathy is free to families via employers and insurers; Atticus uses bank partners). Direct-to-consumer willingness to pay appears weaker for protection, stronger for the one-off "estate settlement" job.

### Cited Findings
- FBI IC3 2025 report (secondary coverage; I could not access the FBI's report): losses for adults 60+ of about $7.7B (+59%), 201,266 complaints (+37%), average loss $38,500, 12,400+ victims lost more than $100,000. Investment fraud was the largest category (about $3.52B), tech support next ($1.04B); crypto-related losses reached about $4.3B for this group; AI-referencing complaints were 3,100+ with $352M in losses. Total complaints across all ages topped 1M with $20.9B in losses. — [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/scams-fraud/fbi-ftc-report-2025-losses), [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/older-adults-lost-billions-to-scammers-2026), [HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/fbi-seniors-cybercrime-2025/), [SOCRadar](https://socradar.io/blog/fbi-ic3-2025-internet-crime-report-10-takeaways/)
- AARP/NAC 2025: 63M US family caregivers (nearly 1 in 4 adults); out-of-pocket spend about $7,200 a year, about 26% of income on average. — [AARP press release July 2025](https://www.aarp.org/press/releases/2025-07-24-new-report-reveals-crisis-point-for-americas-63-million-family-caregivers), [WSMV/AARP Aug 2026](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/08/05/family-caregivers-spend-7000-year-out-pocket-aarp-says/)
- Carefull: $16.5M Series A (Oct 2023, led by Fin Capital; $19.7M total), growth financing from CIBC Innovation Banking (June 2025, amount undisclosed); sells to banks, advisors and insurers; 35+ institutions by 2023; vendor-claimed up to 10x churn reduction (company claim). — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=214226), [CIBC Innovation Banking](https://innovationbanking.cibc.com/en/news/deal/cibc-innovation-banking-provides-growth-financing-to-carefull-to-accelerate-expansion), [CUInsight](https://www.cuinsight.com/press-release/carefull-raises-16-5-million-to-scale-financial-safety-platform-for-older-adults-and-their-families-helping-more-banks-and-financial-advisors-protect-aging-customers-from-fraud-and-money-mistakes/)
- Participation gaps among older adults: only 41% of eligible elderly enrolled in SNAP (2013 figure), 49% of SSI-eligible and 46% of Medicare Savings Program-eligible older Americans enrolled (2024 NCOA-linked report). — [J-PAL](https://povertyactionlab.org/node/2125), [Akron Legal News](https://akronlegalnews.com/editorial/38275)
- Estate settlement: an EstateExec survey (n=1,201) found settlement takes about 16 months on average, about 570 executor hours and about $12,400 in fees; estates of $5M+ took 42 months and 1,167 hours. — [WTOP](https://wtop.com/lifestyle/2021/02/liz-weston-why-you-dont-want-to-be-an-executor/), [Swift Probate roundup](https://www.swiftprobate.com/blog/best-ai-tools-estate-executors)
- Atticus markets estate settlement help (plans from $175 to $499; claims average savings of $14,000 and 450 hours, which is self-reported marketing) and distributes via bank partners (e.g., ATB). Empathy is free to families through employers and life insurers. — [Atticus FAQ](https://www.weareatticus.com/faqs), [ATB Atticus page](https://atb.com/resources/tools/atticus), [Swift Probate roundup](https://www.swiftprobate.com/blog/best-ai-tools-estate-executors)
- I found no pricing or funding information for EverSafe or Kindly; EverSafe is listed as founded in 2014 and based in Columbia, Maryland. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/charlie-3/alternatives-competitors)

### Inferences
- The pain is real and growing (scam losses +59% in one year, AI-enabled), but incumbents sell to banks. A consumer product faces a cold-start trust problem (elders and caregivers must grant account access). B2B2C via banks, credit unions, employers or insurers looks like the viable path, which is hard for a small team to access.
- "Executor autopilot" (death certificate orders, account closures, benefit notifications, final-tax checklist, unclaimed-property search) matches the end-result thesis: a bounded, high-stakes, one-time job with a payer (the estate) that already spends about $12k. It is US-state-specific, though, and probate law varies by state.
- Decumulation planning (drawdown/Medicare/RMD optimisation) is not evidenced in my sources; treat as unvalidated.
- Regulatory friction: high for anything touching account access or advice (GLBA, state elder-abuse reporting laws, RIA rules); lower for document-assist and checklist-style estate tools.

### Gaps
- No data on Kindly or EverSafe pricing, funding or user counts.
- No retiree decumulation product or WTP evidence retrieved.
- No independent evaluation of Atticus or Empathy outcomes (only marketing claims) and no Trust & Will data.
- I could not access the FBI IC3 report directly; figures come via secondary coverage and differ slightly between outlets (59% vs about 60% growth).
- No data on how many caregivers manage a parent's finances specifically (AARP release is about caregiving costs overall).

## 4. Emerging markets (SEA, LatAm, Africa): what is the opportunity and what does the infrastructure look like?

### Takeaway
Infrastructure (Pix, mobile money, GCash) is already giving hundreds of millions of people accounts, so the gap has shifted from access to guidance, trust and advanced products. Super-apps (GCash) own distribution, so a small team likely wins as a layer on top of existing rails (e.g., WhatsApp-native agents) or on the diaspora side, rather than building rails.

### Cited Findings
- Global Findex 2025 (surveys of 145,000+ adults in 141 economies, 2024): about 1.3B adults remain unbanked, with 650M concentrated in Bangladesh, China, Egypt, India, Indonesia, Mexico, Nigeria and Pakistan; Sub-Saharan Africa account ownership rose to 58% (from 49% in 2021); about 40% of adults there hold a mobile money account (27% in 2021); formal savings rose 12 points to 35%. About 900M unbanked adults own a mobile phone. — [World Bank press release 16 July 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/07/16/mobile-phone-technology-powers-saving-surge-in-developing-economies), [BIIA](https://www.biia.com/financial-inclusion-at-record-high-but-1-3-billion-still-unbanked-world-bank-global-findex-2025-report), [BusinessDay](https://clone.businessday.ng/?p=1064786)
- Brazil's Pix (Central Bank Pix management report, 10 Aug 2026, via Brazilian press): almost 80B transactions in 2025 (+25.7%), volume above R$35 trillion (+33.8%), 170M+ users; use in the population reported between 76.4% (Central Bank survey) and 93% of adults (EBANX, a vendor); Pix Automático launched 16 June 2025 (about 600k transactions by December 2025, still small). — [Diário de Pernambuco](https://www.diariodepernambuco.com.br/economia/2026/08/11720778-volume-de-transacoes-via-pix-aumentou-257-em-2025-ante-2024-mostra-bc.html), [EBANX](https://business.ebanx.com/en/press-room/press-releases/pix-to-approach-8-billion-monthly-transactions-as-it-marks-five-year-milestone-ebanx-study-finds), [Movimento Econômico](https://movimentoeconomico.com.br/economia/2026/08/11/chaves-pix-chegam-a-920-milhoes-e-sistema-movimenta-r-35-tri-em-2025/), [DCI](https://www.dci.com.br/economia/pix-bate-recorde-de-transicoes-com-black-friday-de-2025/)
- Philippines: PSE recorded 3.64M stock accounts in 2025 (+27.3%), 99.2% retail, 3.22M held online; GCash's GStocks reports 1.7M to 2M+ users (more than half of online retail accounts) and GFunds 8.8M registered users (company-reported); GInvest minimums as low as P50 for an index fund; account ownership about 58% (SWS survey, Q1 2026) vs 51% (BSP 2025 survey), with 43% holding e-money and 21% a bank account. — [Tribune PH](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/08/30/gcashs-gstocks-ph-now-filipinos-leading-digital-retail-platform-for-stock-investing), [Mynt](https://mynt.com.ph/newsroom/gcash-parent-mynt-grows-gstocks-ph-into-filipinos-leading-digital-retail-gateway-to-the-stock-market), [SunStar](https://www.sunstar.com.ph/davao/business/bsp-notes-rise-in-account-ownership-in-sws-survey)
- WhatsApp-native AI finance in Africa is early-stage and tiny: Nafini (Nigeria, AI lifestyle/payments assistant, rollout phase, June 2025), MyBuddy AI (Kenya, financial education via messaging; MIT Solve 2024 entry, current status unconfirmed), COMBI (Cameroon, diaspora remittances/bill pay on WhatsApp), Zeen (self-described AI neobank, no details). — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/?p=161002), [MIT Solve: MyBuddy AI](https://solve.mit.edu/challenges/2024-global-economic-prosperity-challenge/solutions/88072), [VC4A: COMBI](https://vc4a.com/ventures/combi-intelligence-hub/)
- Félix Pago demonstrates WhatsApp as a real financial channel at scale (6M+ people, $8B), although for US-to-LatAm remittances rather than in-market savings (see section 1).

### Inferences
- Local super-apps (GCash, M-Pesa, Nubank, Mercado Pago) control onboarding and licences; a foreign small team competing in-market must partner or build a thin agent layer. WhatsApp agents for "does the job" tasks (move money, set up recurring savings via Pix Automático/UPI-like autopay) face WhatsApp Business policy and local licensing constraints, which I did not research.
- Diaspora-side products serving emerging-market households (the sender in the US/UK/Gulf, the receiver in Mexico/Philippines/Nigeria) are the best bridge between "global users" and "EM need": the sender pays, the receiver benefits, and the sender's currency and regulatory environment are rich-world.
- Data gap on willingness to pay in low-income EM users suggests monetisation would come from fees/spreads and distribution partnerships, not subscriptions. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on in-market (LatAm/SEA/Africa) willingness to pay for financial advice or AI agents.
- No data on vernacular/voice-first finance adoption or trust metrics; no evidence located on Indonesia, Vietnam, Nigeria (Opay/Moniepoint) or Mexico (Mercado Pago/Nu) specifics.
- No primary data on M-Pesa in 2025/2026 retrieved.
- Pix Parcelado launch status not confirmed.

## 5. Gen Z and first-jobbers: what is the behavioural problem and is there a paid product?

### Takeaway
Young investors show high speculation and heavy reliance on social media, and BNPL late-payment rates among Gen Z are high in most surveys (roughly 39% to 51%). Evidence that Gen Z pays for behavioural finance tools is thin in my sources; the segment looks better suited to free or bundled products than to subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- FINRA Foundation (report released 4 Dec 2025, NFCS 2024 investor survey): investors scored 5.3 of 11 on the financial literacy test; YouTube is the leading source for investing information (over 60% among young people); 61% of those under 35 are influenced by finfluencers vs 26% overall; 29% of under-35s have bought meme stocks vs 2% of those over 55. — [Funds Society summary](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/markets/fewer-new-investors-and-a-decline-among-young-people/)
- Betterment 2026 Retail Investor Survey (n=1,000, vendor-run, methodology questioned by a commentator): 52% of Gen Z investors say they moved money earmarked for investing into sports betting in the past year (14% of millennials, 6% of Gen X, 1% of boomers). — [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/gen-z-diverting-investing-cash-to-sports-betting), [Briefs](https://www.briefs.co/news/sports-betting-emerges-as-a-financial-habit-for-gen-z-invest/)
- BNPL: LendingTree 2025: 41% of US BNPL users paid late at least once in the past year (34% prior year); 64% of Gen Z has used BNPL. Bankrate (May 2025): 49% of BNPL users have had at least one problem; 66% of Gen Z BNPL users have had problems. Motley Fool 2025: 39% of Gen Z BNPL users paid late. Survey definitions differ and one 51% Gen Z figure has no clear source. — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/personal/bnpl-late-survey/), [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/about-half-of-buy-now-pay-later-users-have-experienced-at-least-one-financial-problem/), [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/money/research/buy-now-pay-later-statistics), [Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2025/07/buy-now-pay-later-usage-increasing-delinquencies-rise/)
- Fed SHED 2025 (fielded October 2025, report reportedly published May 2026, per secondary coverage): 63% of US adults could cover a $400 emergency with cash or equivalent (flat), 73% doing okay financially. — [Financer summary](https://financer.com/personal-finance/emergency-fund-statistics/); 2024 report: [Federal Reserve press release 28 May 2025](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20250528a.htm)

### Inferences
- Gambling and options speculation are a behavioural, not informational, problem; the "end-result" product is a commitment/guardrail device (auto-allocation before spend, lock-ups, self-exclusion hooks) rather than a chatbot. The people who most need it are least likely to pay, and platforms profiting from betting will not distribute it. Suitability for a small team is low unless sold B2B (employers, universities, parents, credit unions).
- Regulatory friction: moderate (open banking access and lending-adjacent features), low if it stays advisory.

### Gaps
- No pricing or paid-conversion data for behavioural finance apps (e.g., Cleo, Digit-style products); not researched.
- No non-US Gen Z data (India, UK, Brazil, SEA) beyond the Philippine investor-count data above.
- No independent (non-vendor) study of sports-betting diversion from investing.
- Options-gambling (0DTE) behaviour not researched.

## 6. Household "financial chores" (subscriptions, bills, claims, medical bills, tax, unclaimed property, benefits): is this a winnable end-result category?

### Takeaway
This is the strongest fit for "does the job" AI: bounded tasks with clear dollar outcomes and success-based fees. Evidence: medical-bill errors affect 43% of US adults (KFF 2022), only about 1% of denied claims are appealed (company claim), states return about $4.25B a year of unclaimed property, and EITC underclaiming is about $8B a year. Monetisation precedent is success fees (Rocket Money reportedly takes 30% to 60% of first-year savings on bill negotiation).

### Cited Findings
**Medical bills and insurance denials**
- KFF 2022 Health Care Debt Survey: 57% of adults owed medical or dental debt in the prior five years; 43% of all adults (53% of those with debt) received a bill they thought had an error; 79% disputed it, but 51% of those with debt who received an erroneous bill either failed to fix it or never tried; 32% had a disputed bill sent to collections. KFF estimates at least $220B in US medical debt. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/could-consumer-assistance-be-helpful-to-people-facing-medical-debt/)
- A 2024 USC Schaeffer study (JAMA Health Forum): 20% got a bill they disagreed with or could not afford; among those reporting errors, 74% saw corrections. — [NBC Chicago](https://nbcchicago.com/news/national-international/worth-challenging-medical-bill-cost-study-finds/3536306), [Accounts Recovery](https://www.accountsrecovery.net/2024/09/04/study-shows-medical-bill-disputes-often-lead-to-relief-but-many-dont-reach-out/)
- Counterforce Health (free, grant-funded AI appeal letters): says it has helped about 20,000 people, claims a 70% to 75% approval rate on generated appeals (self-reported), and says under 1% of the 60M+ Americans denied claims appeal. Funding reports conflict ($280K grants vs a $2.47M PennAITech grant). — [Axios Raleigh](https://www.axios.com/local/raleigh/2025/08/20/using-ai-to-fight-back-against-insurance-denials-counteforce), [CBS17](https://www.cbs17.com/news/local-news/wake-county-news/raleigh-organization-helps-thousands-of-americans-appeal-health-insurance-denials-using-ai/amp/), [WECT](https://www.wect.com/2025/07/25/ai-tool-that-helps-patients-battle-insurance-claim-denials-got-its-start-wilmingon)
- Other AI medical-bill startups: Avelis Health ($500K pre-seed from YC, June 2025), Medbill AI/Granted Health (reported $16.25M seed, July 2024), Medcostiq (about $2M, third-party estimate), Resolve ($3.3M seed, 2022); Cedar (provider-side billing platform, $350M raised, $3.2B valuation). — [People of Color in Tech](https://peopleofcolorintech.com/articles/y-combinator-invests-500k-in-ai-startup-helping-americans-save-money-on-medical-bills/), [CB Insights: Medbill AI](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/medbill-ai), [Business Wire: Resolve](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220718005136/en/Resolve-Raises-3.3M-in-Seed-Funding), [MobiHealthNews: Cedar](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/cedar-closes-200m-engaging-patient-payments-paige-updates-its-9-figure-series-c-and-more)

**Unclaimed property and benefits**
- NAUPA: states returned $4,252,648,747 in FY2025 (1 July 2024 to 30 June 2025; 49 member offices reporting), vs over $4.49B in FY2024; NAUPA estimates 1 in 7 Americans (about 33M people) have unclaimed property; MissingMoney.com is the free multi-state search site. — [NAUPA FY25 report](https://unclaimed.org/wp-content/uploads/NAUPA-FY-25-Report.pdf) (cover page verified by PDF text extraction; the cover does not label the unit, but it reads as dollars returned), [Montana Dept. of Revenue](https://mtrevenue.gov/?p=115790), [Delaware News](https://news.delaware.gov/?p=210994)
- I could not source the "$70B+ total held" figure; no search result confirmed it. It is not used here as fact.
- EITC: IRS estimates unclaimed EITC for tax year 2021 of $8.2B ($5.6B from non-filers), 5.7M eligible non-claimants (up from 3.5M in 2019); take-up about 78%. — [IRS SOI paper](https://irs.gov/pub/irs-soi/24rpeitcunderclaimsty2021census.pdf), [Chicago Fed](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/profitwise-news-and-views/2022/millions-tax-refunds-unclaimed), [Tax Policy Center](https://www.taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/do-all-people-eligible-eitc-participate)
- A Pennsylvania trial found that informational mailings nearly doubled SNAP enrollment and mailings plus application help tripled it, meaning that assistance with the application effort matters as much as information. — [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-informational-mailings-snap-enrollment-united-states?lang=es)

**Subscriptions and bill negotiation**
- Rocket Money (formerly Truebill): premium is pay-what-you-want (roughly $6 to $12 per month per a guide); bill negotiation takes a 30% to 60% success fee of first-year savings (third-party report); Truebill's own site claims more than 1M subscriptions canceled (dated, about 2023). I found no revenue or paid-subscriber data. — [Michael Ryan Money](https://michaelryanmoney.com/cancel-truebill-subscription-account/), [Truebill](https://www.truebill.com/cancel-aaa), [Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/4649463-cancellations-faqs)

### Inferences
- The best "end-result" pattern here is: find the money, do the filing, take a success fee (unclaimed property finder-and-claimer; medical-bill audit and dispute; denial appeals; benefit eligibility plus application). These are US-centric, document-heavy, and legally "agent-for" tasks (power of attorney or authorisation forms).
- Unclaimed property is a direct example of a free public database (MissingMoney) with claiming friction; many states cap or regulate "finder" fees, which I have not researched, so treat finder-fee legality as an open question (state-by-state).
- Medical-bill and denial work has the strongest pain evidence and the most crowded early-stage funding (small seed rounds). The Counterforce free model sets a price anchor of zero for appeal letters; a paid product must deliver the full outcome (calls, portal submissions, follow-up) not just a letter.
- For an India-based team, these US chores can be done remotely only if the product is largely automated and compliant with HIPAA (medical bills) and state laws; India-side labour for human review is an arbitrage but adds HIPAA/BAA friction.

### Gaps
- No NAUPA-published total of unclaimed property held (the $70B+ figure is unverified).
- No Rocket Money revenue/subscriber data; no data on subscription-cancellation startup economics, or the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule status.
- No evidence on insurance-claim disputes outside health (auto, home, travel).
- No data on finder-fee legality by state.
- No evidence on benefits enrollment startups' revenue models (e.g., employer benefits navigation).

## 7. Willingness to pay and monetisation: which segments have proof, and what are the candidate gaps?

### Takeaway
Proof of paying customers exists mainly where (a) a transaction fee rides on money already moving (remittance), (b) a licensed professional deliverable replaces a human (tax prep, TurboTax Live, Collective), or (c) a business buys it to protect revenue (Carefull for banks). Consumer subscription for "advice" or budgeting is the weakest monetisation pattern in the evidence (Catch's shutdown, Rocket Money's pay-what-you-want pricing).

### Cited Findings
- Revenue-proven: Wise (about $2.5B net revenue), Remitly ($1.6B revenue, GAAP profitable), Intuit's assisted tax tiers (TurboTax Live +47%). — [Wise FY26](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/wise-class-a--wise/wise-fy26-results/9637901), [Remitly FY25](https://remitly.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/remitly-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results-above), [Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/intuit-turbotax-ai-taxpayer-experience-revenue/749052/)
- Investors are funding diaspora-finance expansion in 2025/2026 (Aspora $53M, Félix $200M, Abound $14M, Belong $5M); see section 1 for citations.
- Failure signal: Catch closed in 2023. — [GPA](https://gpa.net/blogs/americas/us-payroll-and-benefits-fintech-catch-calls-it-a-day)
- Pricing evidence: Collective about $299 per month; Atticus $175 to $499 one-off; Rocket Money success fee of 30% to 60%; CPA cost for PFIC forms of $100 to $300 per form. See sections 2, 3, 6 and 1 for sources.

### Inferences — Candidate gap list (ranked by my judgment of fit for a small India-based team building for global users)
Scoring is my own qualitative judgment: Evidence = strength of sourced pain/market data; Competition = crowdedness; Reg = regulatory friction; Fit = small-team suitability (High/Med/Low).

1. **US-resident NRI "tax and compliance autopilot"** (CAS/NRE/NRO/1099 ingestion; ITR + 8621/FBAR packet; TDS refund; DTAA credit). Who: Indian diaspora (about 34M overseas Indians; US is the top remittance source). Evidence: medium (pain from blogs/fund houses; no sizing). Competition: low-medium (Aspora/Abound adjacent but not filing-focused). Reg: medium (tax-preparer rules, FEMA). Fit: High.
2. **Remittance-linked investing/savings for diaspora** (auto-split each transfer into India MFs/GIFT City deposits or home-country savings). Evidence: strong flow data, strong funding. Competition: high and well-funded (Aspora, Belong, Abound, Félix). Reg: high. Fit: Low-medium (better as partner/API layer than as a licensed entity).
3. **Cross-border inheritance and estate for diasporas** (heirs navigating foreign banks, property, succession certificates). Evidence: weak (no sources). Competition: appears low. Reg: medium (legal practice rules). Fit: Medium; needs validation.
4. **Medical bill audit and dispute (US)**. Evidence: strong (KFF 43% error rate). Competition: medium, mostly pre-seed to seed plus a free nonprofit-like tool (Counterforce). Reg: HIPAA, state debt-collection and authorised-representative rules. Fit: Medium.
5. **Health-insurance denial appeals (US)**. Evidence: strong (about 60M denials, under 1% appealed per company). Competition: free grant-funded rival sets price anchor. Reg: medium. Fit: Medium; monetise via success fee or B2B (employers, patient advocates).
6. **Unclaimed property and benefits "finder-and-claimer"** (MissingMoney search, claim filing, EITC/SNAP/MSP eligibility with application help). Evidence: strong ($4.25B returned a year; about $8B EITC unclaimed). Competition: low in AI-first form. Reg: finder-fee laws vary by state (unverified). Fit: High for automation; revenue per user is small and may require a success fee.
7. **Executor/estate settlement autopilot (US)**. Evidence: medium-strong (about 16 months, 570 hours, $12.4K). Competition: Atticus, Empathy, Trust & Will. Reg: state-by-state probate. Fit: Medium.
8. **Freelancer tax and irregular-income autopilot (US)**. Evidence: strong market size (about 73M to 75M independents), mixed commercial record. Competition: very high. Reg: moderate. Fit: Low-medium unless niche (e.g., non-US freelancers paid in USD, India 44ADA).
9. **Global freelancers' cross-border compliance (India/Philippines/Nigeria freelancers paid in USD: FIRC, GST/LUT, advance tax, W-8BEN)**. Evidence: not found. Competition: unknown. Fit: High-medium for an India-based team; needs validation.
10. **Elder-fraud protection and caregiver financial co-pilot**. Evidence: strong ($7.7B losses; 63M caregivers). Competition: Carefull/EverSafe. Reg: high (account access). Fit: Low for direct-to-consumer; medium via B2B2C partnership.
11. **WhatsApp-native money agent for emerging-market households** (savings set-ups, bill pay, recurring Pix/UPI-style mandates). Evidence: Félix shows channel viability; in-market evidence weak. Competition: super-apps plus well-funded Félix on the diaspora side. Reg: high (local licences). Fit: Low unless partnering.
12. **Subscription and bill-negotiation agent**. Evidence: precedent (Rocket Money) but no financials. Competition: high. Reg: moderate. Fit: Medium; commoditising.
13. **AI consumer tax filing post-Direct-File (US)**. Evidence: Direct File ended for 2026; Intuit dominant; Deduction/april emerging. Competition: very high. Reg: e-file authority. Fit: Low.
14. **Gen Z gambling/BNPL guardrails**. Evidence: moderate (vendor surveys). Competition: low in dedicated form. Monetisation unproven. Fit: Low.

The top three-to-five for the planning team to examine first (by evidence times fit): #1, #6, #9, #4/#5 (as a pair), and #3 as a validation experiment.

### Gaps
- No direct WTP surveys by segment; WTP is inferred from price points and fundings.
- No sizing (TAM) built bottom-up for any gap; sizing would need primary assumptions (counts of households times incidence times fee).
- Regulatory notes in this section are my general knowledge and were not verified against sources (e.g., IRS Circular 230, FEMA/SEBI, HIPAA, state finder-fee laws).
- Singapore, UAE and UK diaspora corridors, and non-Indian diaspora-specific tax pain (Philippines, Mexico, Nigeria), were not researched.
