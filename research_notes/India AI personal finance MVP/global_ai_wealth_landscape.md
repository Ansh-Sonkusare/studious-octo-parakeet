# Global (ex-India) AI-native consumer wealth, investing and personal-finance landscape, as of Oct 2026

Method note: roughly 30 searches/fetches on 2026-10-07. Search-tool summaries were used for most facts, and only a few pages were fetched directly. Labels: (CR) = company-reported, (IND) = independent, press or regulator, (EST) = third-party estimate or tracker, (UNV) = unverified, low-quality source. Several categories had thin or no verifiable data; these are in Gaps. Treat every EST/UNV figure as directional only.

## 1. US: robo-advisors, AI-first advisors, AI money apps, broker AI, AI investing tools and LLM finance

### Takeaway
US scale is held by cash-and-subscription incumbents: Wealthfront (about 75% of revenue from cash-sweep partner-bank fees), Robinhood (Gold subscription plus AI as a Gold upsell) and Betterment. Startups with "AI advisor" branding (Origin, Range, Decade in Brazil) are newer and mostly unproven at scale. The largest consumer-AI money app with a verifiable profit record is Cleo, which monetizes through cash advances and subscriptions rather than advice. Frontier labs are now entering directly: OpenAI launched a bank-linked ChatGPT finance experience (May 2026) and acqui-hired Hiro.

### Inferences
- The only scaled, verifiable economics in US wealth are cash-sweep spread (Wealthfront), subscription-plus-trading (Robinhood Gold) and cash-advance-plus-subscription (Cleo). None of these is "AI advice" revenue.
- Robinhood treats AI (Cortex/Digests) as a Gold retention and upsell feature rather than a standalone product.
- ChatGPT's bank-linking, plus the Hiro acqui-hire, shows horizontal LLMs are moving into the "AI CFO" layer, which compresses the defensibility of thin chat-over-transactions apps. Distribution into licensed execution (custody, advice, credit) is likely the remaining moat.
- Monarch and Copilot appear to be profitable-to-modest subscription businesses at hundreds of thousands of subscribers at most, but no verified figures exist.

## 2. UK and Europe

### Takeaway
Europe's scale winners are execution-led neobrokers (Trade Republic, Scalable) and super-apps (Revolut, Klarna, Bunq) that add AI assistants on top of existing accounts, cards and brokerage. The FCA's targeted-support regime (live 6 April 2026) opens a lane between information and full advice, but uptake is early and AI tools are mostly still in sandbox or pilot.

### Cited Findings

The FCA has approved 7 firms with 23 more applying or in pre-application support (per FCA deputy CEO speech, date not confirmed). ## 4. Patterns: revenue models, AI as feature vs product, retention, failures

### Takeaway
Scaled money comes from float/cash spread, subscriptions bundled with trading or banking, and credit/cash-advance revenue. AI is mostly a feature that raises engagement and cuts servicing cost; AI as the standalone product is unproven, and thin AI-CFO apps have been absorbed (Hiro) or face platform risk (ChatGPT). Verified retention evidence is scarce.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- No evidence found of an AI-advice product earning fees at scale with verified retention. The profitable AI-labeled names (Cleo, Robinhood) monetize credit, subscriptions and trading.
- Platform risk: general LLM assistants with account linking (ChatGPT, Plaid) replicate the read-only "ask about my money" layer. Differentiation likely requires owning regulated action (execution, advice, credit, tax) or proprietary data.
- Rate sensitivity is a structural risk for cash-sweep revenue.
